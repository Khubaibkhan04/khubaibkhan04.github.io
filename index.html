About

Systems first, polish always.

I'm a final-year Computer Game Development student at Air University Islamabad and a working Unity developer. Most of what I build sits under the surface: the netcode that keeps four players in sync, the state machine behind a combo, the AI that decides when a policeman gives chase.

My final-year project, Heist Protocol, is a four-player co-op heist shooter built on Photon PUN — operative classes, a lobby with comms, objective chains and a mission clock. Around it I've shipped a mobile 2D fighting game, an open-world sandbox with a full mission loop, and a VR lab built on Unity's XR Interaction Toolkit.

I care about builds that actually run: profiling draw calls, pooling spawns, and trimming scenes until the frame budget holds on the target device.

Heist ProtocolFinal Year Project

UnityC#Photon PUNCo-op FPS

A four-player co-op PvE heist shooter. A crew picks operatives, drops into a bank, and works a chain of objectives against a mission clock before law enforcement closes in.

What I built

  • Photon PUN networking — room creation, join codes, ready states and synchronised player actions across the crew.
  • Operative classes with per-class stats and passive, active and ultimate abilities that change how a run plays.
  • Objective system driving a live mission board: find the key, crack the locker, clear the police, reach the safe.
  • FPS combat — weapon handling, recoil, reload and ammo, plus enemy police AI that reacts to alarms.
  • Mission HUD — countdown timer, health, objective tracker and weapon panel, all driven from game state.

Arcade Fighter

UnityC#Mobile2D Combat

A fast, arcade-style one-on-one fighter for mobile. Five fighters, four stages, and a round ladder that escalates as you win.

What I built

  • Combat state machine — punch, kick, weapon and block states with hit windows, stun and knockback.
  • Touch control layer — on-screen d-pad and action buttons that map to the same input actions as keyboard play.
  • Round flow — timers, health and super meters, win/lose resolution and progression into the next matchup.
  • Opponent AI that reads distance and pressure to pick between approach, strike and block.
  • Stage & roster pipeline — swappable arenas and fighters driven from data rather than hard-coded scenes.

Open World Sandbox

UnityC#MixamoThird Person

A drivable, walkable city with one complete mission running end to end — from the objective marker to the shootout to the save point.

What I built

  • Third-person controller with camera rig, aim mode, cover-adjacent shooting and seamless vehicle entry.
  • Vehicle system — physics-driven driving, damage and a traffic layer of AI cars that keep the city alive.
  • Police & NPC AI — a wanted-level system where stars escalate the response against you.
  • Mission & save system — objective chain, checkpoint saves and a restore that returns money, weapons and progress.
  • HUD suite — minimap, health, ammo, wanted stars and mission text, all rebuilt from scratch.

VR Lab ExperienceAcademic

Unity 2022.3XR ToolkitC#Room Scale

A room-scale VR environment built for a university XR module — an office and lab interior you can walk, grab and interact with, prototyped against the XR Device Simulator before headset testing.

What I built

  • XR Interaction Toolkit rig — tracked hand models with grab, hover and select interactions on lab props.
  • Locomotion — continuous movement and teleport anchors tuned to keep comfort acceptable indoors.
  • Interactable objects — drawers, cabinets and equipment with socket-based placement.
  • Simulator-first workflow — iterated in the XR Device Simulator so the scene could be tested without a headset in the loop.

Why it matters

  • Proves the same gameplay thinking carries into spatial input, where the interaction model changes completely.
  • Forced a hard performance budget — VR leaves no room for the frame cost a flat build tolerates.
  • Built the habit of designing for comfort, not just for mechanics.
